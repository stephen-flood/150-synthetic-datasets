1. Level (0) — Coin flips and long-run frequency: Simulate n flips; track the proportion of heads over time; visualize convergence; compare to 0.5.

2. Level (0) — Dice rolls and empirical distribution: Roll a fair die n times; produce a frequency table and bar chart; compare to the uniform distribution.

3. Level (0) — Two dice: sum distribution: Simulate sums of two dice; show a histogram; compute P(sum = 7); compare to theoretical probabilities.

4. **Level (1) — Gambler’s ruin: Start with a dollars; win/lose \$1 with probability p; Perform numerical simulations (do lots of trial runs, record data and analyze statistics).  Compare to mathematical expectation of ruin probability and expected time to ruin.**

5. **Level (1) — Urn model (with vs without replacement): Simulate drawing colored balls from an urn using PYTHON LISTS; compare outcomes with/without replacement; estimate probabilities; show how composition changes results.  Compare to theoretical mathematical results**

6. Level (1) — Central Limit Theorem exploration: Sample from a non-normal distribution; record past results in a list; compute sample means across many experiments; show means becoming approximately normal as sample size grows.

7. Level (3) — Random walk and hitting times: Simulate a 1D random walk; estimate probability of hitting a boundary and expected hitting time; visualize sample paths and the hitting-time distribution.

8. Level (3) — Markov chain simulator (board-game movement): Define states and transition probabilities (e.g., simplified board squares); simulate long-run state frequencies; interpret as “time spent” in each state.

9. Level (3) — Queue simulation (waiting lines): Simulate arrivals and service times; measure average wait time and queue length; vary arrival rate to show overload behavior.

10. Level (3) — Inventory / restocking simulation: Model random daily demand; implement a restocking policy; compute stockouts and costs; compare policies via repeated simulations.

11. Level (4) — Stochastic SIR disease model: Individuals transition S→I→R with probabilistic rules; simulate outbreaks; sweep contact/recovery parameters; estimate peak infected and outbreak probability.

12. Level (4) — Agent-based disease spread (grid world): Agents move on a grid; infection depends on proximity; add interventions (masking, distancing, vaccination); compare scenarios.

13. Level (4) — Rumor / information spread on a network: Build a network; simulate spread with transmit/forget probabilities; compare network structures (lattice vs random vs small-world).

14. Level (4) — Vaccination strategy comparison: Using a disease model, compare random vaccination vs targeted vaccination (e.g., high-degree nodes); evaluate reduction in outbreak size.
